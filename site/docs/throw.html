detentThrow()

Drag a card or a sheet past a line and let go: it flies off that way and tells you. Short of the line it springs back, with no bounce.

A card you throw

Drag me past the lineOr flick me left or right.

In play.

.card { translate: calc(var(--x) * 1px) calc(var(--y) * 1px); rotate: calc(var(--tilt) * 1deg); }
.card[data-armed] { outline: 3px solid; }

When it throws

  • Let go past 35% of its width (or its height, up and down) and it is thrown that way.
  • A flick over 600 px a second throws it too, once it has moved at least 40 px. A quick short swipe stays.
  • Short of both, it springs back with no bounce. So does a drag the browser takes back for a page scroll.
  • Past the line the element gets data-armed with the way, so you can show it will go. Once gone it has data-thrown.
  • onThrow fires once per throw. A second throw() during a throw is ignored; one during an undo plays once, when the undo ends.
  • A drag that ends on a button inside the card is not a click on it.
  • With reduced motion it is thrown or put back at once, with the same result.

Keys and screen readers

A drag is never the only way. Give people buttons that do the same, and call throw('left') or undo() from them, as the demo does. That way keys, switch control and screen readers all work, and nothing depends on a gesture.

detentThrow sets no role and takes no focus: the card is yours. Say what happened in your own polite live region, such as "Dismissed. Undo is available."

haptics: true ticks once when the hand takes it past the line. It works on Android, not on iPhone, because Safari has no vibration API. Use it for the clicks that matter, not every step.
