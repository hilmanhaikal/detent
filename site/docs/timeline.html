timeline()

Many animations on one clock. Each step starts at its place in time, and the whole can play, pause, reverse and seek. Its labels are detent marks, so a control can scrub it and settle on them.

Where a step starts

The third argument of add() places the step. Leave it out and the step starts at the end so far.

PositionStarts
500At 500 ms from the start.
'+=200'200 ms after the end so far, so there is a gap.
'-=200'200 ms before the end so far, so it overlaps.
'<'With the step added before it.
'open'At the label open. A label it does not know yet is made at the end so far.

A step with no from starts where the step before it on the same property ends, so seeking anywhere shows the right state. A spring step lasts until it settles, to a 0.5 px rest, so the timeline always has a length.

Scrub it with a detent

Only detent does this. marks() gives the labels as detent marks, so a slider can scrub the timeline and settle on a label, never between two. Drag it, flick it, or tab to it and press the arrow keys.

  2. Packed

  4. Out for delivery
  5. Delivered

The slider says the stop's name, such as "Shipped", because text maps each label time back to its name. The same recipe, with React, is on Recipes.

Accessible by default

  • Reduced motion. play() and reverse() land on the end at once, and every step's onUpdate and onDone still run. seek() still works, so a scrubbing control keeps working with no motion of its own.
  • Hidden tabs. Time stops while the tab is hidden and goes on from the same place.
  • Never layout. Steps move what animate() moves: transform, opacity and custom properties.
  • Keys and screen readers. A timeline has no role. When people control it, give them a real control: a detent slider, as above, or buttons. Keep the final words in the page; the timeline only moves them.

Options

Each step takes the same options as animate(), except motion and signal, which belong to the timeline. A step's delay adds to its position.
